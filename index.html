Field Viewer

A browser sketch of electron and photon fields. Drop packets, watch them move, and check the conservation math when an electron and positron collide.

Lab What you are looking at

What it does

  • Particles are wave packets spread out on fields.
  • Every electron is the same field excitation; only position and momentum differ.
  • Positrons are the same field with opposite charge.
  • When an electron and positron meet, they turn into two photons.

What you are looking at

Field Viewer draws electrons, positrons, and photons as wave packets on two fields in the browser, and prints the conservation math when an electron and a positron annihilate.

  • Two fields on one canvas: the electron field on the top slab, the photon field below it.
  • Three kinds of packet (electron, positron, photon) and three presets (Uniformity, Mirror pair, Annihilation).
  • Physics runs on a fixed 1/120 second step, up to 12 substeps a frame, with units set so c = me = 1.
  • An electron and a positron annihilate once they close to within 0.075 units, in either collinear or center-of-momentum mode, with a 90° default scattering angle.
  • Each annihilation is checked for energy, momentum, and invariant mass squared. A residual has to land under 1e-10 to pass.

A particle here is a wave packet. The electron field covers the whole upper slab of the canvas, and an electron is a localized bump in that field with a position and a momentum. Every electron packet is built from the same template: same mass, same charge, same spin label. Only position and momentum differ. That sounds like a slogan until you can select two packets and read identical numbers off both, which is the main thing I wanted this to show.

The lower slab is the photon field. A positron is the same electron field with the charge flipped, so it sits on the top slab too. When an electron and a positron overlap, the app deletes both, spawns two photon packets below, and prints the bookkeeping: energy in and out, momentum in and out, the invariant mass √s, and a pass or fail badge for each conservation check. If a badge fails, that's a bug in my code.

Controls

  • Presets across the top load Uniformity, Mirror pair, or Annihilation.
  • Pick a spawn tool in the bottom dock, or press 1 through 4 for select, electron, positron, photon. Drag on a slab to launch a packet in that direction.
  • Space plays and pauses. S steps one frame while paused. R resets the preset.
  • Click a packet to open the inspector and steer its momentum by hand.
  • The time slider slows everything down, and the traces toggle leaves trails.
  • Settings switches annihilation between collinear and center-of-momentum mode, with a scattering angle for the COM case.

What does the simulation skip?

No field equations are integrated here, and there are no spinors. Motion is deterministic and units are set so c = mₑ = 1. The About tab lists every other simplification I know about, including Pauli exclusion and pair production.

Field Viewer is free to use in the browser. There is no account, no install, and nothing to buy.
